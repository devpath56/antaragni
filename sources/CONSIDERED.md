# Every thinker and school considered

Nothing raised is discarded. A source that was weighed and not taken records a boundary of the
corpus, and the reasoning is worth more than the absence.

Status: **held** (in the seven streams) · **apparatus** (explains another source, not scored) ·
**bench** (viable, not selected) · **cut** (reasoned out) · **context** (raised but never a
candidate).

## Eastern

| name | region · era | status | reasoning |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Tiruvaḷḷuvar**, *Tirukkuṟaḷ* | Tamil · ~1st c. BCE–5th c. CE | **held** | anchor. Power and sustained intimacy. Virtue binds power |
| **Viṣṇuśarman**, *Pañcatantra* | India · ~200 BCE–300 CE | **held** | *nīti* fables. Animals carry no biography, so cases arrive pre-universalised |
| **Gautama**, *Nyāya Sūtra* | India · ~2nd c. CE | **held** | means of knowledge, fallacies, and the full bad-faith catalogue |
| **Bharata**, *Nāṭyaśāstra* | India · ~200 BCE–200 CE | **held** | rasa as a production function for audience effect |
| **Pāṇini**, *Aṣṭādhyāyī* | India · ~5th–4th c. BCE | **held** | narrowed to *utsarga* / *apavāda* and rule-conflict precedence — a formal theory of the exceptions field |
| **Kabir** | India · 15th c. | **held** | frame-break strand only. Renunciation excluded — incompatible with the householder frame |
| **Zeami**, *Fūshikaden* | Japan · c. 1400 | **held** | training curriculum by performer age, with the losing years written in. *Hiden* — secret until 1909 |
| **Abhinavagupta** | Kashmir · c. 950–1016 | **apparatus** | supplies *sādhāraṇīkaraṇa*, the mechanism under Bharata. Prose commentary, so not scored |
| **Patañjali**, *Yoga Sūtra* | India · ~2nd–4th c. CE | **bench** | *abhyāsa* and the nine obstacles are a ready-made situation index. Displaced from the striving stream by Zeami |
| **Bhartṛhari**, *Nīti Śataka* | India · ~5th c. | **bench** | one verse states the whole striving domain: the best are struck again and again and do not abandon what they began |
| **Bhagavad Gītā** | India · ~2nd c. BCE | **cut** | answers a settled question, and its doctrine is action without attachment where the operator's position is intense attachment. See `quartets/0002` |
| **Kauṭilya**, *Arthaśāstra* | India · ~3rd c. BCE–3rd c. CE | **cut** | redundant with *Poruṭpāl*. Its only marginal contribution is removing the moral constraint, which was a deliberate choice to keep |
| **Ānandavardhana**, *Dhvanyāloka* | Kashmir · ~850 | **cut** | the *kārikās* are verse but the prose *vṛtti* is load-bearing |
| **Rājaśekhara**, *Kāvyamīmāṃsā* | India · ~10th c. | **cut** | closest to a literal creativity manual, but prose and only partially surviving |
| **Bhartṛhari**, *Vākyapadīya* | India · ~5th c. | **cut** | deep on how meaning arises, thin on anything executable |
| **Vasugupta / Utpaladeva**, Spanda and Pratyabhijñā | Kashmir · ~9th c. | **cut** | a metaphysics of creative agency with low operational depth |
| **Śilpa-śāstra**, *Mānasāra*, *Mayamata* | India · ~5th–11th c. | **cut** | strongly operational, welded to building and icon-making |
| **Umāsvāti**, *Tattvārtha Sūtra* — Jain *tapas* | India · ~2nd–5th c. | **cut** | voluntary hardship as method is conceptually apt; the purpose is karmic and the frame ascetic |
| **Dhanurveda**, *Malla Purāṇa*, Kalaripayattu | India · various | **cut** | the subcontinent's martial texts are technique manuals, not training philosophy |
| **Yoga Vāsiṣṭha**, *Vidura Nīti* | India · various | **bench** | Vidura overlaps *Poruṭpāl*; the Vāsiṣṭha is enormous and idealist |
| **Musashi**, *Go Rin no Sho* + *Dokkōdō* | Japan · 1645 | **bench** | contest doctrine from someone undefeated in sixty duels. The *Dokkōdō* is 21 lines and worth an afternoon |
| **Takuan Sōhō**, *Fudōchi Shinmyōroku* | Japan · 17th c. | **bench** | the mind that does not fixate — performing under pressure |
| **Yagyū Munenori**, *Heihō Kadensho* | Japan · 1632 | **bench** | sword master to the shoguns; strategy as governance, the advisor-to-power seat |
| **Dōgen**, *Shōbōgenzō* | Japan · 13th c. | **cut** | practice *is* realisation is the deepest answer to daily practice without outcome — and it is monastic, which fails the householder gate as Kabir's renunciation did |
| **Shuhari** | Japan · pedagogy | **context** | follow the form, break it, leave it. Plugs into *utsarga* / *apavāda* as a developmental sequence |
| **Hagakure**, Yamamoto Tsunetomo | Japan · c. 1716 | **cut** | nostalgic, death-fixated, thin on method, and carrying later militarist appropriation |
| **Shintō**, *Kojiki*, *Nihon Shoki* | Japan · 8th c. | **cut** | mythological chronicles. No aphoristic practical corpus, no decision situations, no exception-marking |
| **Jātaka tales** | Buddhist · ~3rd c. BCE on | **context** | animal fables older than or contemporaneous with the *Pañcatantra*; direction of borrowing unresolved |
| **Tilak**, *Gītā Rahasya* | India · 1915 | **context** | reads *karma-yoga* as energetic action against the quietist reading. Filed as the Gītā's reopening condition |
| **Śaṅkara · Rāmānuja · Madhva · Jñāneśvar** | India · various | **context** | the Gītā's commentary tradition; evidence its scholarship is not popular even where its reception is |
| **Krishnamacharya · K. Pattabhi Jois** | Mysore · 20th c. | **context** | Ashtanga Vinyasa borrowed Patañjali's name, not his content. A practice, not a source |
| **Rabindranath Tagore** | Bengal · 1861–1941 | **context** | not a source. *Gitanjali* 35 supplies the seven seats and the governing criterion |
