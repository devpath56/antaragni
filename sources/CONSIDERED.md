# Every thinker and school considered

Nothing raised is discarded. A source that was weighed and not taken records a boundary of the
corpus, and the reasoning is worth more than the absence.

Status: **held** (in the seven streams) · **apparatus** (explains another source, not scored) ·
**bench** (viable, not selected) · **cut** (reasoned out) · **context** (raised but never a
candidate).

## Eastern

| name | region · era | status | reasoning |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Tiruvaḷḷuvar**, *Tirukkuṟaḷ* | Tamil · ~1st c. BCE–5th c. CE | **held** | anchor. Power and sustained intimacy. Virtue binds power |
| **Viṣṇuśarman**, *Pañcatantra* | India · ~200 BCE–300 CE | **held** | *nīti* fables. Animals carry no biography, so cases arrive pre-universalised |
| **Gautama**, *Nyāya Sūtra* | India · ~2nd c. CE | **held** | means of knowledge, fallacies, and the full bad-faith catalogue |
| **Bharata**, *Nāṭyaśāstra* | India · ~200 BCE–200 CE | **held** | rasa as a production function for audience effect |
| **Pāṇini**, *Aṣṭādhyāyī* | India · ~5th–4th c. BCE | **held** | narrowed to *utsarga* / *apavāda* and rule-conflict precedence — a formal theory of the exceptions field |
| **Kabir** | India · 15th c. | **held** | frame-break strand only, read in the **Guru Granth Sahib recension** — the one Kabir text with stable numbering (aṅg) and a commentary tradition, so the only one that passes the Authority gate. The detachment strand is scoped out because it sits on the axis rejected in `quartets/0002`, not because Kabir was a renunciate: he was a householder weaver whose sharpest polemic is against external renunciation. The Gurus' interpolated replies in *Salok Kabir Jī* are an in-text exception generator. Reader should know this is Guru Arjan's Kabir (1604) |
| **Zeami**, *Fūshikaden* | Japan · c. 1400 | **held** | striving stream. Training curriculum by performer age, with the losing years written in. *Hiden* — secret until 1909 |
| **Abhinavagupta** | Kashmir · c. 950–1016 | **held** — as Bharata's concept field | *Abhinavabhāratī* on NS 6–7. Reworks Bhaṭṭa Nāyaka's *sādhāraṇīkaraṇa* into his own rasa theory (suggestion, removal of the seven obstacles); holds that every rasa including *raudra* and *karuṇa* is relished, not suffered — which gives each rasa a built-in exception. Earlier filed as unscored "apparatus" on the grounds it is prose commentary; that cites the form rule `CRITERIA.md` retired, and `CRITERIA.md` itself says concept and exceptions come from commentary. Restored 2026-09-12 per the operator's original intent ("fill gaps and reinforce the other pillars"). Scope extended under `decisions/0006` to the *Tantrasāra* — the agent-self doctrine in his own voice — and the *Gītārthasaṃgraha* as a reopener on the Gītā |
| **Patañjali**, *Yoga Sūtra* | India · ~2nd–4th c. CE | **bench** | *abhyāsa* and the nine obstacles are a ready-made situation index. Displaced from the striving stream by Zeami. Tension with the operator's Ashtanga interest closed under `decisions/0006`: Trika ranks Pātañjala yoga a lower stage (*Pratyabhijñāhṛdayam* 8) and replaces its definition (*Śiva Sūtra* 1.12). No conflict if the Ashtanga is the physical practice; the *Vijñāna Bhairava* is the practice text that passes every gate |
| **Bhaṭṭa Nāyaka**, *Hṛdayadarpaṇa* (lost) | Kashmir · c. 900 | **context** | originator of *sādhāraṇīkaraṇa* and the three functions (*abhidhā*, *bhāvakatva*, *bhojakatva*). Survives only in Abhinavagupta's quotation and rebuttal. Named because the README credits the mechanism and a ledger claiming completeness omitted him |
| **Kātyāyana**, *vārttikas* on Pāṇini | India · ~3rd c. BCE | **context** | the documented proof that Pāṇini's rule set needed a human outside it — every *vārttika* is a boundary the sūtras missed. Re-filed from bench to context under `decisions/0006`: he is the Eastern *evidence* for Gödel's claim, not a rival bank |
| **Patañjali**, *Mahābhāṣya* | India · ~2nd c. BCE | **context** | the grammarian, distinct from the *Yoga Sūtra* author. The precedence machinery stream 7 is scoped for (*paratva*, *nityatva*, *antaraṅga*, *apavāda*) lives here and in the later *paribhāṣā* tradition (Nāgeśa), not in the *Aṣṭādhyāyī* |
| **Guru Nanak** and the **Ādi Granth** | Punjab · 1469–1539; compiled 1604, closed 1708 | **context** | not a bank; the **text** for the Kabir bank. The only source in the corpus that passes the Authority gate cleanly: 1430 aṅgs, standard pagination, dense ṭīkā tradition. *Siddh Gosṭ* (aṅg 938–946) is a frame-break dialogue against renunciate yoga in verse. Cost named: it is the living Guru of a religion, and rejecting its verses in public costs more than rejecting the Gītā did. The saint-*soldier* strand is the *Dasam Granth*, not this text |
| **Guru Gobind Singh**, *Dasam Granth* | Punjab · 1690s | **cut** | the soldier half of saint-soldier — *Chandi di Var*, *Zafarnama*. Authenticity disputes and 1690s, outside the ancient bank |
| **Dadu Dayal** and the **Dadupanthi anthologies** | Rajasthan · 1544–1603 | **context** | the other western Kabir family. The *Kabir-granthāvalī*'s *aṅga* headings (topical sections) are the best situation index over Kabir; no stable numbering across editions, so it indexes but does not cite. *Pañc-vāṇī* and Rajjab's *Sarvāṅgī* are multi-voice sant anthologies — structurally what this repo is |
| **Bhartṛhari**, *Nīti Śataka* | India · ~5th c. | **bench** | one verse states the whole striving domain: the best are struck again and again and do not abandon what they began |
| **Bhagavad Gītā** | India · ~2nd c. BCE | **cut** | answers a settled question, and its doctrine is action without attachment where the operator's position is intense attachment. See `quartets/0002` |
| **Kauṭilya**, *Arthaśāstra* | India · ~3rd c. BCE–3rd c. CE | **cut** | redundant with *Poruṭpāl*. Its only marginal contribution is removing the moral constraint, which was a deliberate choice to keep |
| **Ānandavardhana**, *Dhvanyāloka* | Kashmir · ~850 | **cut** | the *kārikās* are verse but the prose *vṛtti* is load-bearing. Note: this reason cites the retired form rule, the same ground on which Abhinavagupta was wrongly filed. Reopen if his restoration holds |
| **Rājaśekhara**, *Kāvyamīmāṃsā* | India · ~10th c. | **cut** | closest to a literal creativity manual, but prose and only partially surviving |
| **Bhartṛhari**, *Vākyapadīya* | India · ~5th c. | **cut** | deep on how meaning arises, thin on anything executable. Note under `decisions/0006`: he is the bridge from Pāṇini to Trika's theory of speech — *parā, paśyantī, madhyamā, vaikharī* — and Abhinavagupta leans on him. Stays cut as a bank; read if stream 7's concept field runs dry |
| **Vasugupta / Utpaladeva / Kṣemarāja** — Spanda, Pratyabhijñā, the *Śiva Sūtras*, *Pratyabhijñāhṛdayam* | Kashmir · 9th–11th c. | **context** — the ground | the metaphysics under stream 4, adopted by operator ruling (`decisions/0006`). Earlier cut as "low operational depth": wrong on inspection — the *Śiva Sūtras* are 77 numbered sūtras with three commentaries and pass every gate; the *Vijñāna Bhairava* is 112 practices. Not a bank; the ground the banks stand on. Reading queue in `sources/DEEPER.md` |
| **Śilpa-śāstra**, *Mānasāra*, *Mayamata* | India · ~5th–11th c. | **cut** | strongly operational, welded to building and icon-making |
| **Umāsvāti**, *Tattvārtha Sūtra* — Jain *tapas* | India · ~2nd–5th c. | **cut** | voluntary hardship as method is conceptually apt; the purpose is karmic and the frame ascetic |
| **Dhanurveda**, *Malla Purāṇa*, Kalaripayattu | India · various | **cut** | the subcontinent's martial texts are technique manuals, not training philosophy |
| **Yoga Vāsiṣṭha**, *Vidura Nīti* | India · various | **bench** | Vidura overlaps *Poruṭpāl*; the Vāsiṣṭha is enormous and idealist |
| **Musashi**, *Go Rin no Sho* + *Dokkōdō* | Japan · 1645 | **bench** | contest doctrine from someone undefeated in sixty duels. The *Dokkōdō* is 21 lines and worth an afternoon |
| **Takuan Sōhō**, *Fudōchi Shinmyōroku* | Japan · 17th c. | **bench** | the mind that does not fixate — performing under pressure |
| **Yagyū Munenori**, *Heihō Kadensho* | Japan · 1632 | **bench** | sword master to the shoguns; strategy as governance, the advisor-to-power seat |
| **Dōgen**, *Shōbōgenzō* | Japan · 13th c. | **cut** | practice *is* realisation is the deepest answer to daily practice without outcome — and it is monastic, which fails the householder gate as Kabir's renunciation did |

| **Hagakure**, Yamamoto Tsunetomo | Japan · c. 1716 | **cut** | nostalgic, death-fixated, thin on method, and carrying later militarist appropriation |

| name | region · era | status | reasoning |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Robert Caro**, *The Power Broker*, *Years of LBJ* | US · 1974–2012 | **held** | power stream. Forensic where Valluvar is prescriptive. *Power doesn't corrupt, power reveals*. | **Thomas Schelling**, *Strategy of Conflict* | US · 1960 | **held** | contest stream. Win by credible commitment, against the *Pañcatantra*'s deception |
| **Edward Bernays**, *Propaganda* | US · 1928 | **held** | moving-people stream. Ratified under the agency lens (`decisions/0006`): the audience as object against Abhinavagupta's *sahṛdaya* — the audience as agent. Brecht refuses adbhuta outright, a different fight; on the bench |
| **Nietzsche**, *Thus Spoke Zarathustra* | Germany · 19th c. | **held** | striving stream. | **Karl Popper** | Austria/UK · 20th c. | **held** | reasoning stream. Never confirm, only fail to refute — against Nyāya's positive *pramāṇa* theory |
| **Kurt Gödel** | Austria · 1931 | **held** | generating stream. Stronger second bank for stream 4 than Bernays, who is a theory of who should move an audience rather than how |
| **Anders Ericsson**, *Peak* | Sweden/US · modern | **bench** | deliberate practice. Empirical grounding for whether losses compound without feedback |
| **Robert Cialdini**, *Influence* | US · 1984 | **bench** | more usable than Bernays, less revealing |
