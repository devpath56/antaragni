# 0002 — Resilience added as a domain

Settled 2026-09-12.

Seventh situation added: **enduring repeated defeat**. It was missing, and it is not a minor one —
running a platform shipping production agents is sustained adversity, not a sequence of contests.

## It is already covered — no new source needed

| source | where | what it gives |
|---|---|---|
| **Valluvar** | *Poruṭpāl* ch. 62, unfailing exertion | effort that does not slacken outlasts fate |
| **Valluvar** | *Poruṭpāl* ch. 63, not being destroyed by adversity | a dedicated chapter — laugh at trouble, and there is nothing else that defeats it |
| **Pañcatantra** | Book 4, *Labdhapraṇāśa* — the loss of what has been gained | losing ground you had already won, and recovering it |
| **Bharata** | *vīra rasa* and its underlying state, *utsāha*; *dhṛti* among the transitory states | a taxonomy of the internal states of endurance, so they can be named rather than felt |
| **Kabir** | the frame-break strand | a person who has already lost everything cannot be threatened with loss |

Chapter numbers to verify against a primary edition.

## The real gap

All of the above is **endurance of the unchosen** — misfortune arrives, and you are not destroyed
by it.

None of these sources theorizes **chosen repeated defeat as a training method**: deliberately
entering a contest you will lose, daily, against people with a structural advantage, because losing
under controlled conditions is what builds the capacity.

That is a different mechanism. Endurance is about surviving what happens to you. This is about
selecting your defeats on purpose and metabolising them.

The nearest classical doctrine is *abhyāsa* — sustained, uninterrupted practice — but it sits in
sources outside this corpus and is framed toward stillness rather than contest.

**Provisional ruling:** for this one domain the primary source is first-hand — a decade of martial
training before university, showing up daily to be beaten by taller and stronger opponents. Write
those quartets from experience and cite them as such. An honest first-hand case beats a borrowed
one, and the exceptions field is the place to be ruthless about what did *not* transfer.
