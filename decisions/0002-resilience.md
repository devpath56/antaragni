# 0002 — Resilience added as a domain

Settled 2026-09-12.

Seventh situation added: **enduring repeated defeat**. It was missing, and it is not a minor one —
running a platform shipping production agents is sustained adversity, not a sequence of contests.

## It is already covered — no new source needed

| source | where | what it gives |
|---|---|---|
| **Valluvar** | *Poruṭpāl* ch. 62, unfailing exertion | effort that does not slacken outlasts fate |
| **Valluvar** | *Poruṭpāl* ch. 63, not being destroyed by adversity | a dedicated chapter — laugh at trouble, and there is nothing else that defeats it |
| **Pañcatantra** | Book 4, *Labdhapraṇāśa* — the loss of what has been gained | losing ground you had already won, and recovering it |
| **Bharata** | *vīra rasa* and its underlying state, *utsāha*; *dhṛti* among the transitory states | a taxonomy of the internal states of endurance, so they can be named rather than felt |
| **Kabir** | the frame-break strand | a person who has already lost everything cannot be threatened with loss |

Chapter numbers to verify against a primary edition.

## The real gap

All of the above is **endurance of the unchosen** — misfortune arrives, and you are not destroyed
by it.

None of these sources theorizes **chosen repeated defeat as a training method**: deliberately
entering a contest you will lose, daily, against people with a structural advantage, because losing
under controlled conditions is what builds the capacity.

That is a different mechanism. Endurance is about surviving what happens to you. This is about
selecting your defeats on purpose and metabolising them.

## Correction to the paragraph above

As first written this said the nearest doctrine "sits in sources outside this corpus", which was
true of the six sources chosen and false as an implication about the tradition. The subcontinent
covers this domain well. The gap was in the source list, not in the literature. Three candidates:

| candidate | what it supplies |
|---|---|
| **Bhagavad Gītā** | a warrior collapsing before a fight he does not want, argued back into acting with a claim on the action and none on the result. Argues *against* withdrawal, so it passes the householder gate outright |
| **Yoga Sūtra**, Book 1 + *kriyā yoga* | *abhyāsa* and *vairāgya*: practice takes root only when sustained a long time, unbroken, with sincerity — plus a numbered list of the nine things that break a practice, which is a ready-made situation index |
| **Bhartṛhari**, *Nīti Śataka* | one verse that states the whole domain: the low never begin for fear of obstacles; the middling begin and stop when struck; the best, struck again and again, do not abandon what they began. Same *nīti* genre as Valluvar and the Pañcatantra |

**Ruling, then reversed same day.** The Gītā was admitted, scoped to the action-without-attachment
material, and then rejected.

## Reversal — the Gītā is out

Stated objection: it is a text for the masses despite its cast being warriors and elites.

That objection as stated does not hold. The Tirukkuṟaḷ is more widely distributed in Tamil Nadu —
schools, government buses, public statuary — than the Gītā is in India, and its commentary
tradition is no denser. A popularity criterion removes Valluvar before it removes anything else.

**The reason that does hold is structural.** The Gītā's central question is *should I act at all*.
Its premise is a man trying to leave the field. That question is settled here — this casebook is
for someone already in the contest, and it does not need seven hundred verses arguing him into it.

**Criterion this produces, added to `CRITERIA.md`:** a source must not spend its length answering a
question already settled. Judge the question a text asks, not the size of its readership.

Note the criterion is about scope, not quality. The Gītā carries commentaries by Śaṅkara,
Rāmānuja, and Abhinavagupta himself. Its reception is popular; its scholarship is not. Irrelevant
if the question it answers is one that is already closed.
