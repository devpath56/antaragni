# 0001 — Entering a contest you will lose

**Seat:** striving · **Source:** first-hand · **Verdict:** accepted, 2026-09-12

## Situation

You are entering a contest you will lose, against opponents holding a structural advantage, and you
will do it again tomorrow, and for years.

## Heuristic

Enter, and stay attached to winning — but to winning on a horizon the scoreboard in the room does
not measure.

## Concept

Showing up to be beaten is not sustained by detachment. It is sustained by **attachment to a
terminal goal far enough out that today's result is an input to it rather than a verdict on it.**

The loss is not tolerated. It is *invested*. Opponents who are older, taller and stronger hold an
advantage that is **temporary**, and the correction taken from each defeat compounds into the period
when it is no longer true. You are not indifferent to losing. You are indifferent to *when* you win.

This is the opposite of acting without attachment to results. It is attachment intensified and
aimed further out.

## Exceptions

1. **Losses must be survivable.** A spar is bounded and reversible. A company is not. Where a defeat
   is terminal, this inverts into recklessness. This is where the transfer out of the training hall
   fails first, and it fails hardest for a founder.
2. **Losses must actually compound.** Being beaten without coaching, feedback, or a model of what
   went wrong is attrition, not training. Repetition alone compounds nothing.
3. **The terminal goal must be right.** Compounding toward a wrong target produces a highly trained
   person aimed at nothing.
4. **Deferral must stay falsifiable.** "Long horizon" can quietly become permanent immunity from
   measurement. If no result at any horizon would count as evidence you were wrong, this has stopped
   being a strategy and become a way of never being scored.
5. **The opponent's advantage must be the kind that decays.** Age and size converge. Capital,
   network and incumbency do not. Where the advantage is permanent, the arithmetic borrowed from
   sport does not run.
